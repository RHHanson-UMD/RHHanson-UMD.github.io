# Attribution and rights concerns

RHHanson-UMD Learning Lab is an independent student-created educational project. It is not an official University of Maryland website and is not endorsed by the university, instructors, textbook publishers, or external reference providers.

## What belongs on the public website

- Original explanations, independent practice, and original code-generated illustrations may be published after mathematical and factual review.
- Third-party references should normally be linked and cited, rather than copied. Their copyright and license terms remain with their respective owners.
- Unreleased homework, private lectures or notes, original answer keys, identifiable student information, and credentials must not be copied into public branches, issues, pull requests, workflows, or Pages artifacts.
- Released official solutions do not automatically have redistribution permission. Verify both release and publication rights before publishing homework-specific solutions.
- This notice is **not** a claim of fair-use authorization, a content license, or a legal determination.

## Report a concern

For attribution, copyright, privacy, or public-content concerns, [open an issue in the main site repository](https://github.com/RHHanson-UMD/RHHanson-UMD.github.io/issues/new). Include the URL/path of the affected content, a brief description of your concern, and a link to the original source when public.

**GitHub issues are public.** Do not include personal contact information, confidential academic documents, unreleased solutions, or private correspondence. If the supporting information is private, open a minimal issue requesting a nonpublic contact route without disclosing the material.

## How maintainers should handle concerns

1. Acknowledge the concern in the issue where appropriate without reposting confidential content.
2. Locate the cited site content and review the relevant source, original creation history, publication permission, and license attribution.
3. If a credible concern cannot be promptly resolved, remove or temporarily hide the affected public content while reviewing it; do not retain sensitive material in public Git history.
4. Correct the attribution, replace copied content with an original independent example, obtain documented permission where appropriate, or keep the material removed.
5. Confirm the final published Pages artifact reflects the resolution and close or update the public issue with a non-sensitive summary.

No response deadline or legal conclusion is promised. Where legally appropriate, escalate difficult copyright questions to qualified counsel. Protect private student and instructor materials throughout review.
